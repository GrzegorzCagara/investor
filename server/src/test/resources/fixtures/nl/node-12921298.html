Gamgertamig data from ongoing Phase 1b studies suggest potential to retain remission after B cell reconstitution, maintained in the absence of background therapy for autoimmune cytopenias, consistent with an “immune reset”

Lakefront to host investor conference call on Monday, December 14 at 8 AM ET

Mechelen, Belgium; November 4, 2026, 15.01 CET – Lakefront Biotherapeutics NV (Euronext & NASDAQ: LKFT) today announced the acceptance of a poster presentation at the 2026 American Society of Hematology (ASH) Annual Meeting taking place on December 12-15 in New Orleans, LA.

“Prior to acquiring Ouro together with Gilead, we recognized gamgertamig’s potential to change clinical practice for patients with chronic and refractory autoimmune cytopenias, based primarily on results from investigator-sponsored trials. It is exciting to explore the predictability of robust responses, and the potential for immune reset-type activity, in results from company-sponsored trials sponsored by both Lakefront and our China-based partner, Keymed,” said Eric Hedrick, Chief Medical Officer of Lakefront. “We are looking forward to sharing updated data at ASH.”

Title: Initial global clinical experience with gamgertamig (CM336/OM336), a BCMAxCD3 T-cell engager, in relapsed/refractory autoimmune cytopenias (AICs) in two parallel ongoing studies (NCT07083960, NCT07175493)
Presenter: Phil Choi, MBBS, PhD

Preliminary data suggest potential to retain remission after B cell reconstitution, maintained in the absence of background therapy for AIC, consistent with an “immune reset”.

Abstract Highlights 

  
Patients33 patients with autoimmune cytopenias (AICs) received gamgertamig, of which
  • 22 patients with immune thrombocytopenia (ITP)
  • 11 with autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) (warm AIHA=9, cold agglutinin disease (CAD)=1, mixed AIHA=1)
DosingData are reported across three dose cohorts. All patients received short course subcutaneous dosing over a maximum of three weeks

Median disease duration
  • ITP cohorts: 8.4 years (range: 1.7-46.5)
  • AIHA cohorts: 3.7 years (range: 1.5-26.5)
Responders
  • ITP: 18/20 (90%) achieved response [platelet count ≥30 × 10^9/L and at least double from baseline];
    • 2/3 (67%), 4/5 (80%) and 12/12 (100%) patients in the low, mid, and high dose cohorts, respectively,
    • with normalization of platelet count (≥150 × 10^9/L) observed in 17/18 (94%) responders
  • AIHA: All response-evaluable patients were in the mid and high dose cohorts and all achieved a response, defined as a ≥2 g/dL increase in hemoglobin from baseline: 6/6 (100%) and 3/3 (100%), respectively
Median time to response from first injection
 
  • ITP: 11 days (range: 4-85)
  • AIHA: 22 days (range: 4-81)
Follow-up
  • ITP: Among 20 response-evaluable patients, median follow-up was 141 days (range: 29-253)
  • AIHA: Among 9 response-evaluable patients with AIHA (wAIHA=7, CAD=1, mAIHA=1), median follow-up was 85 days (range: 57-198)
Durability of response
  • ITP: Of the 18 responders, 15 (83%) had an ongoing response at the most recent follow-up
  • AIHA: Of the 9 responders, 8 (89%) had an ongoing response at the most recent follow-up.
Safety
  • Grade 1 cytokine release syndrome (CRS) occurred in 6/33 patients (18%)
  • No Grade ≥2 CRS events were observed
  • No cases of immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) or other neurotoxicity were observed
  • Infection was observed in 10/33 (30%) patients; most infections were Grade 1 or 2, all managed successfully.

About Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP)
Immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) is a rare, autoantibody-driven disease that results in the premature destruction of platelets, causing thrombocytopenia and an increased risk of bruising and bleeding. ITP affects approximately 120,000 people in the U.S. Beyond bleeding risk, people with chronic ITP often experience significant fatigue and reduced quality of life, along with side effects associated with long-term corticosteroid or immunosuppressive therapy. Despite currently available treatments, significant unmet need remains for therapies that provide safe and durable responses.

About Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA)
Autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA) is a serious hematologic disease characterized by the production of autoantibodies that target and destroy red blood cells. AIHA affects an estimated 40,000 to 50,000 people in the U.S. Warm AIHA (wAIHA) is the most common form of AIHA, accounting for approximately 80% of cases, while cold agglutinin disease (CAD) is less prevalent. Common symptoms include fatigue, anemia, jaundice, splenomegaly, and, in severe cases, thromboembolism and cardiovascular complications. Despite recent advances in treatment, substantial unmet need remains for additional therapies that provide durable responses and improved long-term disease control.

About Gamgertamig
Gamgertamig is an investigational BCMAxCD3 bispecific T cell engager for the treatment of autoantibody-driven immune-mediated disease. Gamgertamig has been granted Orphan Drug Designation and Fast Track Designation by the U.S. FDA for certain autoimmune diseases. Gamgertamig is expected to enter registrational studies in 2027. Gamgertamig is in-licensed from Keymed Biosciences, which owns the rights to develop the program in Greater China.
