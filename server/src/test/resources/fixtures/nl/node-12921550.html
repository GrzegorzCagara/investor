ArcelorMittal reports third quarter 2026 results

05 Nov 2026 07:00 CET

Issuer

ARCELORMITTAL SA

Luxembourg, November 5, 2026 - ArcelorMittal (referred to as “ArcelorMittal” or the “Company” or the "Group") (MT (New York, Amsterdam, Paris, Luxembourg), MTS (Madrid)), the world’s leading integrated steel and mining company, today announced results1 for the three-month and nine-month periods ended September 30, 2026.

3Q 2026 key highlights:

Safety focus: Protecting employee health and safety is a core Company value. The multi-year safety transformation continues to progress, focused on embedding execution discipline and delivering consistent safety performance across all regions. LTIF rate of 0.57x in 9M 2026, compared with 0.68x in 9M 2025.
Higher margins demonstrate structural improvement in business performance: EBITDA of $2.0 billion in 3Q 2026, (+34% year-on-year) and EBITDA per tonne improved to $155/t (+$44/t year-on-year), remaining significantly above historical averages. Performance continues to benefit from strategic growth investments, optimized assets and diversified market exposures. In Europe, flat steel shipments increased sequentially, counter to normal seasonal patterns, reflecting the early impacts of the enhanced EU trade measures.
Underlying profitability improved: 3Q 2026 reported net loss of $0.4bn included impairment2 and exceptional items3 related to the idling of ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih (Ukraine). Adjusted net income4 in 3Q 2026 increased to $0.9 billion (adjusted basic EPS of $1.15), compared with an adjusted net income of $0.7 billion (adjusted basic EPS of $0.90) in 2Q 2026.
Strong financial position: Net debt increased to $10.3bn4 on September 30, 2026 as compared to June 30, 2026, reflecting $0.4bn of shareholder returns and a temporary $0.5bn net working capital investment during the quarter. Liquidity7 remains robust at $8.7bn, and the Company’s positive free cash flow outlook for 2026 and beyond remains unchanged.
Positive underlying cash generation: The business generated $0.5bn of underlying free cash flow in 9M 2026, after investing $1.1bn in strategic growth projects and excluding the temporary $2.5bn working capital investment. The 9M 2026 investment in working capital of $2.5bn is expected to largely unwind in 4Q 2026, supporting a healthy cash generation and continued returns to shareholders and lower net debt.
Capital return policy continues to create value for shareholders: During 9M 2026, the Company returned $1.1bn to shareholders through dividends and share buybacks. Since September 2020, the fully diluted share count has been reduced by approximately 39%5.

Strategic focus:

Outlook across the near, medium and long term remains positive: Conditions going forward continue to strongly benefit from the regionalization of steel markets and strengthening policy support. Medium-term earnings growth is underpinned by the delivery of the Company’s strategic growth projects. Long-term prospects remain supported by structural demand tailwinds and a strong pipeline of high-return growth opportunities in attractive markets.

European market fundamentals continue to strengthen: Enhanced EU trade measures are beginning to rebalance European steel market fundamentals, reducing import penetration and supporting higher domestic capacity utilization. Following the implementation of the new tariff rate quota (TRQ), ArcelorMittal has brought back all its European blast furnaces into operation, underpinning the preservation of skilled jobs and local supply chains. While higher energy prices have created a temporary cost headwind, steel prices are adjusting to the higher cost environment, supported by lower import volumes and normalizing inventories, ensuring that the European industry can earn its cost of capital. The Company expects flat shipment growth to accelerate in the fourth quarter (a double-digit year-on-year increase), with Group shipments in 2H 2026 expected to exceed those in 1H 2026.

Further policy support remains a prospect: The additional policy measures to expand protection downstream could further strengthen European industrial competitiveness and with it domestic steel demand, building on the positive impact already seen from enhanced steel trade measures.

Strategic growth projects support higher EBITDA and ROCE: The Company’s portfolio of organic growth projects and completed M&A is expected to increase EBITDA potential by approximately $1.8bn6 from 2026 and beyond, unchanged from previous guidance (excluding the Tubarão and potential Pecém downstream projects in Brazil, for which project economics and EBITDA potential will be provided as part of the regular project update alongside 4Q 2026 results). Strategic growth investments target higher-margin products and attractive returns, supporting structurally higher EBITDA, free cash flow and ROCE. 2026 capex guidance remains unchanged at $4.5bn-$5.0bn, including $1.7bn-$1.9bn of strategic capex.

Safety and sustainable development

Health and safety:

Protecting employee health and safety is a core Company value. The multi-year safety transformation continues to deliver measurable improvements, with an LTIF rate of 0.57x in 9M 2026 vs. 0.68x in 9M 2025.

In 2026, the safety transformation program progressed into its implementation and scale-up phase, focused on embedding execution discipline and delivering consistent safety performance across all regions.

Key milestones included implementation of the upgraded H&S Competency Leadership Model (~80% of operational leaders assessed), with assessment results informing enhanced Safety Academy content to build leadership capability, rollout of the enhanced Contractor Health & Safety Management Standard (with ~20% of sites completing implementation and first audits), and broader deployment of digital safety solutions to strengthen hazard identification and risk management.

Sustainable development highlights:

  • European trade defence and industrial policy is continuing to broaden: Policymakers are assessing further measures to address trade diversions downstream, with TRQ scope expansion reviews scheduled, political alignment on extending CBAM to downstream products, and growing trade support for industrial sectors (e.g. Automotive policy measures and Industrial Accelerator Act could favour EU-produced steel through local content rules). These developments have the potential to strengthen European industrial competitiveness and support domestic European steel demand.
     
  • The energy transition remains an attractive opportunity for ArcelorMittal, enabling the Group and its customers to decarbonize through investments in renewables, electrical steels for EVs and low-carbon building solutions, with EAF investments playing a key role in the decarbonization of our steelmaking footprint.
     
  • European EAF projects advancing: Gijón (1.1Mt long products) and the Sestao expansion (1.6Mt) are on track for commissioning in 4Q 2026, with ramp-up through 2027, while the 2Mt flat products EAF at Dunkirk has moved into execution. Together, these projects are expected to add approximately 3.9Mt of EAF capacity by 2029, supporting both decarbonization and long-term value creation.

Analysis of results for 3Q 2026 versus 2Q 2026

Sales decreased by 1.8% to $16.5 billion in 3Q 2026 as compared to $16.8 billion in 2Q 2026, primarily reflecting a 2.1% decrease in steel shipments.

Operating loss in 3Q 2026 was $131 million, including impairment charges of $852 million and exceptional charges of $293 million related to the idling of ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih (Ukraine) operations. Excluding these charges, the underlying operating performance was broadly stable compared to $1,055 million in 2Q 2026.

EBITDA in 3Q 2026 decreased to $2,023 million as compared to $2,064 million in 2Q 2026. Lower results in Europe (higher carbon and energy prices with lagged benefit of higher steel prices yet to be reflected), Sustainable Solutions (seasonal factors) and Mining segments (lower market prices and higher freight rates) were partly offset by improved performance in Brazil. 

Net interest expense increased to $181 million in 3Q 2026 from $136 million in 2Q 2026 and includes $37 million additional one-off charge associated with a joint-venture.

Foreign exchange and other net financing charges amounted to a gain of $10 million in 3Q 2026 as compared to a loss of $286 million in 2Q 2026. 3Q 2026 includes $0.1 billion non-cash mark-to-market gain on derivatives related to energy contracts and lower foreign exchange impacts.

An income tax expense of $350 million in 3Q 2026 compares with an income tax expense of $175 million in 2Q 2026. 3Q 2026 income tax expense includes $170 million deferred tax asset reduction following the idling of Ukraine operations.

Net loss in 3Q 2026 was $391 million, or $0.52 loss per share, as compared with net income of $683 million, or $0.90 earnings per share, in 2Q 2026. Reported results were impacted by impairment and exceptional charges related to the idling of ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih in Ukraine. Excluding these items, adjusted net income4 increased to $868 million in 3Q 2026 from $683 million in 2Q 2026, with adjusted basic earnings per common share increasing to $1.15 in 3Q 2026 from $0.90 in 2Q 2026.

Net cash provided by operating activities during 3Q 2026 amounted to $665 million, as compared to $961 million in 2Q 2026, and included a working capital investment of $492 million as compared to $457 million in 2Q 2026. Capex for 3Q 2026 was $1,065 million, including $361 million spent on strategic growth projects, as compared to $1,102 million in 2Q 2026. Free cash outflow of $442 million, together with shareholder returns of $390 million, resulted in net debt of $10.3 billion as at September 30, 2026, as compared to $9.5 billion as at June 30, 2026.

Recent development

  • On September 25, 2026, ArcelorMittal announced that it is unable to safely and sustainably operate ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih (Ukraine) following multiple missile strikes. ArcelorMittal Kryvyi Rih has continued to operate in an increasingly challenging environment since the onset of the conflict. Following a fundamental change in the safety risk profile, together with severe pressure on the logistics system, the Company took the difficult decision to idle operations, with the safety of employees remaining the overriding priority. Since the onset of the war, ArcelorMittal has provided over $700 million of financial support to sustain the business and maintain continuity of operations. Going forward the future cash spend is expected to be limited (including 4Q 2026) and focused on asset preservation, security and environmental compliance while maintaining restart optionality.

Appendix 5: Terms and definitions

Unless indicated otherwise, or the context otherwise requires, references in this earnings release to the following terms have the meanings set out next to them below:

Adjusted basic EPS: refers to adjusted net income divided by the weighted average common shares outstanding.

Adjusted net income: refers to reported net income less impairment items and exceptional items and related tax impacts and one-off tax charges.

Apparent steel consumption (ASC): calculated as the sum of production plus imports minus exports.

Average steel selling prices: calculated as steel sales divided by steel shipments.

Cash and cash equivalents: represent cash and cash equivalents, restricted cash and short-term investments.

Capex: represents the purchase of property, plant and equipment and intangibles. The Group’s capex figures do not include capex at the JVs level (i.e. AM/NS Calvert until June 18, 2025 and AMNS India).

Crude steel production: steel in the first solid state after melting, suitable for further processing or for sale.

Depreciation: refers to amortization and depreciation.

EPS: refers to basic or diluted earnings/(loss) per share.

EBITDA: defined as operating income plus depreciation, impairment items and exceptional items and income (loss) from associates, joint ventures and other investments (excluding impairments and exceptional items if any).

EBITDA/tonne or margin: calculated as EBITDA divided by total steel shipments.

Exceptional items: income / (charges) relate to transactions that are significant, infrequent or unusual and are not representative of the normal course of business of the period.

Free cash flow (FCF): refers to net cash provided by operating activities less capex less dividends paid to minority shareholders. The term free cash outflow is used when the difference is negative (i.e. negative free cash flow)

Foreign exchange and other net financing income/(loss): include foreign currency exchange impact, bank fees, interest on pensions, impairment of financial assets, revaluation of derivative instruments and other charges that cannot be directly linked to operating results.

Gross debt: long-term debt and short-term debt.

Impairment items: refers to impairment charges net of reversals.

Income from associates, joint ventures and other investments: refers to income from associates, joint ventures and other investments (excluding impairments and exceptional items, if any).

Investable cash flow: refers to net cash provided by operating activities less maintenance/normative capex.

Iron ore reference prices: refers to iron ore prices for 62% Fe CFR China. Pricing is generally linked to market price indexes and uses a variety of mechanisms, including current spot prices and average prices over specified periods. Therefore, there may not be a direct correlation between market reference prices and actual selling prices in various regions at a given time.

Kt: refers to thousand metric tonnes.

Liquidity: defined as cash and cash equivalents (included cash held as part of assets held for sale) plus available revolving credit facilities

LTIF: refers to lost time injury ("LTI") frequency rate equals lost time injuries per 1,000,000 worked hours (own personnel and contractors) and includes fatalities; an LTI is an incident that causes an injury that prevents the person from returning to his/her next scheduled shift or work period.

Maintenance/normative capex: refers to capital expenditures outside of strategic capital expenditures projects (and includes cost reduction plans and environment projects as well as general maintenance capital expenditures).

Mt: refers to million metric tonnes.

Net debt: long-term debt and short-term debt less cash and cash equivalents (including cash and cash equivalents held as part of assets held for sale)

Net interest expense: includes interest expense less interest income.

Operating results: refers to operating income/(loss).

Operating segments: North America segment includes the Flat, Long and Tubular operations of US, Canada and Mexico; and also includes all Mexico mines. The Brazil segment includes the Flat, Long and Tubular operations of Brazil and its neighboring countries including Argentina, Costa Rica, Venezuela; and also includes Andrade and Serra Azul captive iron ore mines. The Europe segment includes Flat and Long operations, and through October 30, 2025, included Bosnia and Herzegovina captive iron ore mines; Sustainable Solutions division includes Downstream Solutions and Tubular operations of the European business and our renewables operations in India. The Others segment includes the Flat, Long and Tubular operations of Ukraine and South Africa, the captive iron ore mines in Ukraine, holding companies and intragroup stock margin eliminations. Mining segment includes iron ore operations of ArcelorMittal Mines Canada and ArcelorMittal Liberia.

Own iron ore production: includes total of all finished production of fines, concentrate, pellets and lumps and includes share of production.

Price-cost effect: a lack of correlation or a lag in the corollary relationship between raw material and steel prices, which can either have a positive (i.e. increased spread between steel prices and raw material costs) or negative effect (i.e. a squeeze or decreased spread between steel prices and raw material costs).

ROCE (Return on capital employed): refers to operating income, excluding impairment and exceptional items, plus income from associates, JVs and other investments (excluding impairments and exceptional items, if any), minus income taxes (excluding one-off tax charges) divided by the average equity plus net debt for the period.

Shipments: information at segment and Group level eliminates intra-segment shipments (which are primarily between Flat/Long plants and Tubular plants) and inter-segment shipments respectively. Shipments of Downstream Solutions are excluded.

Underlying free cash flow: Refers to free cash flow less change in working capital

Working capital change (working capital investment / release): refers to movement of change in working capital - trade accounts receivable plus inventories less trade and other accounts payable.
